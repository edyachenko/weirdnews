## #6 - Fed trade threat
According to CNN, on September fourth, twenty twenty-six, with a pivotal Federal Reserve decision looming, Trump threatened to cease trading with America's top trading partners unless the Fed lowered interest rates. Not adjusted tariffs. Not renegotiated deals. Stop trading. Over an interest rate. That is like threatening to stop paying your electric bill unless your neighbor turns his thermostat down. The Vice President and the Treasury Secretary reportedly joined the pressure campaign too, because nothing says independent central bank like the entire executive branch shouting at it in unison. Bold economic strategy. Truly the plan of a very serious person.

The Trump-Tucker Carlson feud, simmering since twenty twenty-five over foreign policy disputes, kept boiling. ## #4 - Convention fact-check
At the Republican midterm convention in Dallas, Trump told the crowd there were, quote, no empty seats, end quote, in the arena. According to CNN's fact-check, that was visibly untrue, empty seats and all, on camera, for anyone who wanted to count them. He also claimed almost every item was getting cheaper while prices kept climbing, and insisted he had kept every single promise from the campaign trail. Three claims, three fact-checks, zero accuracy. At some point, lying about how full a room is stops being spin and starts being performance art.

## #3 - Doonbeg "I built it"
On a trip built around the Irish Open at his own Doonbeg resort, Trump told reporters, quote, I built it twenty-two years ago, right on the ocean, end quote. He did not build it. He bought the resort around twenty fourteen. The course was designed by Greg Norman and opened back in two thousand two, years before Trump owned so much as a doormat there. Rory McIlroy, diplomatically, said Trump's presence could only bring more attention to the tournament. Which is one way to describe a man taking credit for a golf course like he personally dug the bunkers with his own hands.

## #2 - The 2026 coin
The US Mint rolled out a commemorative dollar coin for America's two hundred fiftieth birthday, featuring Trump's portrait and the dates seventeen seventy-six to twenty twenty-six, making him the first sitting president in a century to appear on US currency. Rolls of twenty-five went for sixty-one dollars, bags of a hundred for a hundred fifty-four fifty. Columnist Catherine Rampell joked the coin marks liberty's birth date as seventeen seventy-six, and its death as twenty twenty-six. Here's the part critics zeroed in on: federal law says only a dead person's portrait belongs on US currency, and a separate 2005 law bars living presidents specifically from this coin series. The Mint's answer is a loophole: a 2020 law banning living portraits from the back of these anniversary coins never says a word about the front, which is exactly where Trump's face is. Technically legal, say the lawyers who wrote it. Deeply on-brand, says everyone else.

[v2 fact-check: CORRECTED. v1 called this "an old, unwritten rule" -- wrong on both counts. It's a written law (31 U.S.C. 5114(b); Presidential $1 Coin Act of 2005), and the coin exploits a specific obverse/reverse gap in the Circulating Collectible Coin Redesign Act of 2020, not a norm nobody enforces.]

Speaking at the Pentagon on the twenty-fifth anniversary of September eleventh, Trump said, quote, we will always remember the victims and families of September eleventh, end quote, then paused for a good three seconds, and said, quote, twenty-O-one. Very famous date, unfortunately, end quote. On the anniversary of nine eleven. The date the ceremony was literally named after. Earlier that week he had also claimed firefighters rescued him near the Twin Towers as a building collapsed, a claim reported by the Daily Beast, which the White House dismissed by saying the outlet was, quote, really grasping at straws, end quote. Grasping at straws, or just quoting the man directly. You decide.
